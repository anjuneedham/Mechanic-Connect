Before you call anyone

  • Get off the carriageway first. Hazards on, wheels turned away from traffic. Nothing else matters more.
  • Note the warning lights that are actually lit, and whether they came on before or after. Photograph the cluster.
  • Check the obvious three: fuel gauge, temperature gauge, battery light.
  • Look underneath for anything dripping, and note the colour.
  • Listen once more. Silence, a single click, rapid clicking and a slow crank are four different faults.
  • Note where you are as a landmark and a direction of travel.

How to describe the fault

  • When it happens — cold start, after 20 minutes, only over bumps, only braking.
  • What it does — whines, knocks, grinds, shudders, hesitates, pulls left.
  • What changes it — goes away above 40, worse in reverse, stops when I brake.
  • What happened before — drove through water, hit a pothole, just had work done.

Eight questions to ask before work starts

  1. What do you think it is, and how confident are you?
  2. Is there a diagnostic charge, and does it come off the repair?
  3. What is the total — parts and labour separately?
  4. Are the parts new, aftermarket or used?
  5. What happens if you open it and find more?
  6. How long, and can I collect the old parts?
  7. Is there a warranty on the work, and for how long?
  8. Can you put that in a message before you start?

Six signs the job is going sideways

  • The price moves and nobody called you before it moved.
  • The whole assembly must go, with no explanation of which part failed.
  • Nothing is in writing, and requests to write it down are deflected.
  • The old parts cannot be produced and no reason is offered.
  • New faults appear right after a repair to an unrelated system.
  • You are pressed to decide now, without a second opinion.
