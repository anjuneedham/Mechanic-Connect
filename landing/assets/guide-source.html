01 · At the roadside

Before you call anyone

Two minutes here changes the conversation. A mechanic who arrives to a clear description quotes on the fault. One who arrives to “it just stopped” quotes on the risk.

  • Get off the carriageway first. Hazards on, wheels turned away from traffic, triangle out if you have one. Nothing below matters more than this.
  • Note the warning lights that are actually lit, and whether they came on before or after the trouble started. Photograph the cluster.
  • Check the obvious three: fuel gauge, temperature gauge, and whether the battery light is on. A surprising share of call-outs end here.
  • Look under the vehicle for anything dripping, and note the colour — clear, brown, red, green or black tells a mechanic different things.
  • Listen once more. Try the ignition and pay attention: silence, a single click, rapid clicking and a slow crank are four different faults.
  • Note where you are in a way someone else can find: a landmark and a direction of travel beats a road name.

02 · Vocabulary

How to describe the fault

You do not need the technical word. You need the when. Fill in these four and you have told a mechanic more than most callers do.

Say thisBecause it separates
When it happens
cold start, after 20 minutes, only over bumps, only braking		 A fault that appears only when hot is a different search from one that appears only when cold.
What it does
whines, knocks, grinds, shudders, hesitates, pulls left		 Rotating noises, impact noises and vibration point at different systems.
What changes it
goes away above 40, worse in reverse, stops when I brake		 Tells the mechanic which part is loaded when the noise appears.
What happened before
drove through water, hit a pothole, just had work done		 Recent history is the single most useful thing you can offer, and the most often left out.
Record it. A ten-second voice note of the noise, taken while it is happening, is worth more than any description. Send it with the job.

03 · Before work starts

Eight questions to ask

  1. What do you think it is, and how confident are you? A straight answer distinguishes a diagnosis from a guess.
  2. Is there a diagnostic charge, and does it come off the repair? Agree this before anyone opens anything.
  3. What is the total — parts and labour separately? One lump number cannot be checked or compared.
  4. Are the parts new, aftermarket or used? All three are legitimate. Not being told which is not.
  5. What happens if you open it and find more? Ask them to stop and call you at an agreed figure rather than continue.
  6. How long, and can I collect the old parts? Asking for the old parts back is normal and costs nothing.
  7. Is there a warranty on the work, and for how long? Get it in writing, even a WhatsApp message.
  8. Can you put that in a message before you start? A written quote is the whole of your protection.

04 · Money

Reading a quote

A quote you can check contains:

  • Each part named, with its condition
  • Labour shown separately, with hours
  • Any diagnostic or call-out fee
  • What is not included
  • A date, and how long the price holds

Parts — ask this for each:

TypeAsk
New / OEMIs it the maker's part or the maker's supplier's?
AftermarketWhich brand, and what warranty comes with it?
UsedWhat is it off, and is it tested or sold as seen?

05 · Warning signs

Six signs the job is going sideways

  • The price moves and nobody called you before it moved.
  • You are told the whole assembly must go, with no explanation of which part failed.
  • Nothing is in writing, and requests to put it in writing are deflected.
  • The old parts cannot be produced and no reason is offered.
  • New faults appear immediately after a repair to an unrelated system.
  • You are pressed to decide now, on the spot, without a second opinion.
One of these is a question. Three is a second opinion. You are entitled to stop, pay for the diagnostic you agreed to, and take the vehicle elsewhere.
